3-phase AC induction motor

Three AC currents, each 120° apart, run through the stator coils. Their magnetic fields add up to one field that rotates. That changing field induces current in the rotor bars (Faraday's law), which creates torque, so the rotor spins up and settles a little slower than the field. The difference is called slip. Change the sliders and watch the graphs respond.

This is a browser version of my physics project, which was written in Python with VPython. The equations and the RK4 solver are the same; the view here is flat (top-down) instead of 3D.

Stator coils and field arrows: red = phase A, green = phase B, blue = phase C, black = total field. Rotor bars show a dot (cyan) when the induced current points toward you and a cross (magenta) when it points away.
